From Raw Data to Model: An End-to-End Data Science Pipeline in Python

A series of eight labs from the Master's in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Tecnológico de Monterrey, course TC5053 – Data Science and Analytics (April to July 2026). Each one tackles a different problem with a public dataset, and together they cover the full cycle: extraction with pandas and SQL, exploratory analysis, preprocessing, feature engineering, dimensionality reduction, and regression models built as scikit-learn pipelines. The best models reached an R² of 0.963 predicting diamond prices and 97.4% accuracy classifying tumors, with no false positives.

My role

I completed the pandas, SQL, exploratory analysis and preprocessing labs (1 to 4) individually. The feature engineering, PCA and regression labs (5 to 8) were developed in a three-person team; the PCA lab was submitted from my repository. All of them received the top grade.

The data science cycle

Process

  1. Extraction and cleaningLoading data with pandas (types, dates, aggregations) and SQL queries with joins against a MySQL database via SQLAlchemy.
  2. ExplorationDistributions, correlations and pattern visualization with matplotlib and seaborn.
  3. PreprocessingHandling missing values and outliers, encoding categorical variables, and scaling.
  4. Feature engineering and PCACreating and selecting variables, and reducing to the components that explain more than 90% of the variance.
  5. Modeling and evaluationComparing encodings, scalers, polynomial terms and transformations (Yeo-Johnson) inside scikit-learn pipelines, measured with RMSE, R², recall, precision and accuracy.

Conclusions

  • Moving from the baseline to the polynomial model cut the price error in half (from 1,532 to 749 USD RMSE).
  • In the classifier, PCA and the Yeo-Johnson transformation raised recall from 0.905 to 0.929 without losing precision. Recall was prioritized because in a medical diagnosis a false negative is the most serious error.
  • The instructor highlighted the reusable pipelines and the step-by-step justification of each model's performance.

Scope and limitations

These are academic labs using well-known public datasets, designed to practice each stage of the cycle, not production solutions. The models are linear and logistic; work with more complex models (ensembles, neural networks) continues in other courses of the master's program.

What it shows

  • Full data cycle: from extraction (CSV and SQL) to model evaluation, with reproducible code in notebooks.
  • Thoughtful preprocessing: choosing encodings, scaling and transformations based on the data, and measuring their effect.
  • Dimensionality reduction: using PCA to handle highly correlated variables.
  • Metrics that match the cost of errors: prioritizing recall when a false negative is the worst outcome.
  • ML best practices: scikit-learn pipelines that chain preprocessing and model to prevent data leakage.
