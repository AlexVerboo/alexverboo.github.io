From Data to Strategy: Sales Analytics & Data Storytelling

Team project for the Data Visualization course (May–June 2026). Using the sales history of Numismática México, the team analyzed when, what and where the company sells, and turned those findings into an executive presentation with recommendations on advertising, inventory and geographic focus.

  • Data Storytelling
  • Data Visualization
  • KPIs
  • Exploratory Analysis
  • PowerPoint
  • Gamma

My role

I built the data storytelling narrative and the final executive presentation, and translated the team's analytical findings into concrete business recommendations.

The challenge

Numismática México wanted a clearer view of its sales behavior to guide business decisions. The analysis answered five questions:

  • When do the main sales increases happen?
  • Which products bring in most of the revenue?
  • Where is demand concentrated geographically?
  • How can seasonality be leveraged with advertising campaigns?
  • Where should commercial efforts be focused?

The data was the case's historical sales records (dates, revenue, products and regions), focused on 2021 and 2022.

Key findings

  • 64%of revenue comes from just 3 products.
  • 53%of sales are concentrated in 4 states: State of Mexico, Mexico City, Jalisco and Veracruz.
  • 50%of 2022 sales were driven by advertising.

A clear opportunity was also found in November and December, with room to improve during Buen Fin (Mexico's main shopping weekend). Figures are those reported in the academic deliverable, not results from a validated experiment.

From findings to strategy

Recommendations

  • Focus advertising campaigns on the products that contribute most to revenue.
  • Prioritize the four states with the highest sales concentration.
  • Increase advertising during Buen Fin and the last week before Christmas.
  • Plan inventory according to historical demand patterns.
  • Measure campaign impact against an organic-sales baseline and watch for cannibalization between the two.

Process

  1. Business questionsIdentify commercial needs and frame the analysis questions.
  2. Exploration and KPIsReview the sales history and choose revenue, time, product and geography indicators.
  3. AnalysisTrends and seasonality, revenue concentration by product, and distribution by state.
  4. InterpretationTurn each finding into a business opportunity.
  5. Data storytellingBuild an executive narrative that leads from data to decision, based on the principles of Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic).

What it shows

  • Data storytelling: turning analytical results into an executive narrative that connects evidence, context and decisions.
  • Business sense: translating percentages into concrete marketing, inventory and geographic actions.
  • Executive communication: presenting findings clearly for decision-makers, not just analysts.
  • Analytical thinking: questioning patterns (for example, organic vs. ad-driven sales) before recommending.
